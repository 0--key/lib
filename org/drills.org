* Anecdotes

** Blonde and King-Kong #1
A common version of the joke goes as follows: A blonde woman is asked
about the chances of meeting King Kong in the jungle.

    She replies, "Oh, the chances are about 50/50".  When asked how
    she arrived at that conclusion, she explains, "Well, you either
    meet him, or you don't!"

The joke is a classic example of a "blonde joke" (a subgenre of jokes
often relying on stereotypes about intelligence) that uses flawed,
binary logic to calculate probability, ignoring the extremely low
likelihood of the event actually occurring.

** Blonde and King-Kong #2

The anecdote you are referring to is a variation of a "blonde" joke
that typically plays on a stereotype about intelligence or
statistics. The punchline revolves around a misunderstanding of
probability.  A common version of the joke is:

    A blonde is told that the likelihood of her meeting King Kong is a
    million to one. She replies, "Oh no! Why me?"

The joke's humor comes from her reaction, which implies she thinks the
odds of "a million to one" mean she is highly likely to meet him
(specifically her, as in, the single unlucky person out of a million),
rather than understanding that the odds mean it is highly
improbable. The premise uses the pop culture connection of King Kong's
attraction to blonde women, as seen with the character Ann Darrow in
the films.

** An Unemployed Engineer

An unemployed engineer decided to open a clinic and puts a sign
outside that reads: "A cure for your ailment guaranteed at $500; we'll
pay you $1,000 if we fail."  A doctor, believing he can trick the
engineer and earn $1,000, visits the clinic with various fabricated
ailments.

- When the doctor claims he has lost his sense of taste, the engineer
  gives him a few drops of what turns out to be gasoline. The doctor
  immediately identifies it as gasoline, and the engineer declares his
  sense of taste is restored, charging $500.

- Attempting to recoup his money, the doctor returns a few days later,
  claiming amnesia. The engineer again instructs the nurse to prepare
  medicine from "box 22," and the doctor recognizes this is the same
  "gasoline" from before. The engineer then announces the doctor's
  memory is back and charges another $500.

- Still determined, the doctor returns later complaining of poor
  eyesight. The engineer asks for medicine from "box 11," but the
  nurse brings "box 22". The doctor exclaims that's the box with the
  gasoline, and the engineer pronounces his vision restored,
  collecting a final $500.
